What shapes the price

Page count is one part of it. Custom layouts, writing help, image preparation, interactive features, moving old content, and connecting services can also affect the work. A one-page site with complex features may involve more than several simple pages.

Start with a smaller project

A menu update, a landing page, or a focused refresh can be a sensible first step. If your budget is limited, we can discuss the essentials and what might be added later as a separate project.

Pricing is negotiable

Share your budget openly. We can discuss the amount of work and whether a smaller scope will fit. There is no automatic fixed price or guaranteed discount; the final fee and included work are agreed before building.

What your quote should explain

Expect a description of the pages and features, what content you supply, included revisions, expected timing, and the payment schedule. Domain, hosting, subscriptions, and any paid assets should be identified separately.

When the idea changes

Extra pages or new features may change the estimate. We’ll discuss the extra work and price before proceeding, so a new request doesn’t quietly become an unexpected bill.

Your handy checklist

  • Choose the pages and features that matter most
  • Say whether you need help with text, photos, or moving content
  • Review the scope and separate recurring costs
Good things to ask

A few questions, answered.

Can you quote without a budget?

We can still discuss your requirements. A budget range simply helps narrow down a practical starting point.

Is asking for an estimate a commitment?

No. We discuss the proposal first. Work begins under the project agreement and its payment requirements.

A real person, a little guidance

Still figuring it out? That’s okay.

I’m Luis. If you need help finding a domain or understanding something website-related, just mention it in your email. Advice is free; purchases and project work are discussed separately.
